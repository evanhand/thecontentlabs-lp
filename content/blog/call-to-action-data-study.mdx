Creators argue about calls to action constantly. "Asking for a follow looks desperate." "Always end with a question." "Never sell in the video." We stopped arguing and counted.

We pulled **4,914 short-form videos** from **76 creators** in The Content Labs dataset, had the analysis engine record what each video asked the viewer to do (nothing, follow, comment, click or buy) and when it asked (never, middle, end), and compared each video against that same creator's normal views. Measuring against the creator's own median means the result is about the ask, not the size of the account.

<Highlight title="Follow beats comment, middle beats end">
Videos that asked for a **follow** ran at **1.25x** the creator's normal views across 1,226 videos. Videos that asked for a **comment** ran at **1.03x**. Videos that asked people to **click or buy** ran at **0.82x**. And when the ask came in the **middle** of the video, it ran at **1.37x**, with **40.3%** doubling and **20.9%** doing five times the creator's normal. The same ask at the **end** ran at **0.96x**.
</Highlight>

The follow ask is the only one that beats saying nothing. It ran at 1.25x across 1,226 videos from 18 creators, doubled 36.9% of the time and did 5x 18.2% of the time. No ask at all was a normal video (1.05x, 2,223 videos). The comment ask, the one most advice recommends, was also a normal video (1.03x, 1,016 videos), with 30.0% doubling.

The link or buy ask is the one to notice. It ran at 0.82x across 449 videos from 21 creators, with a 25.2% breakout rate and average views of 35,574, less than a third of the follow-ask average. Promo as a content category told the same story at 0.83x. The audience can feel the pitch coming and leaves before it arrives.

This is the bigger finding. An ask in the middle of the video ran at 1.37x across 905 videos, the strongest single bucket in this study, with 40.3% doubling and 20.9% at 5x. An ask at the end ran at 0.96x across 1,882 videos, below saying nothing at all (1.06x).

The reason is simple. Most viewers never reach the end. An ask at the end is heard by the loyal minority who were going to follow anyway. An ask in the middle, placed just after the first payoff, is heard by everyone who is still interested and has just been given a reason.

## Why the follow ask does not hurt

The follow ask runs at 1.25x partly because of who uses it. Creators who ask for the follow are usually the ones making the kind of video people want more of: stories (1.26x), news (1.42x), takes with a number behind them. The ask is not magic. It is a habit of creators whose videos already give a reason, and it turns the reason into a follower instead of a one-time view.

<Callout title="The practical rule">
Find the first moment in the script where the viewer thinks "that was good." Put the follow ask right there, one line, and keep going. Cut the end ask. If you need to sell, put it in the caption or the next video, not in the one you want to travel.
</Callout>

## Caveats

The sample skews toward commentary, sports and news, where a follow is the natural ask. In a product niche the link ask may be the whole point of the video; the data says it costs reach, not that it costs money. And the timing finding depends on having a first payoff early enough to hang the ask on, which brings this back to structure.

## How we ran this

Every video was processed by The Content Labs analysis engine, which watches the video, reads the transcript and records what the video asks the viewer to do and at what point, using one definition for every video. We then divided each video's views by that creator's median views over the period, so the numbers are relative to each creator's normal rather than raw counts. Buckets with fewer than 60 videos are not reported. The dataset is 9,289 analysed videos from 76 creators across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X; 4,914 had a recorded ask and a measured baseline.

## FAQ

**How big was the dataset?**
4,914 short-form videos with a recorded call to action, from 76 creators on The Content Labs, drawn from 9,289 analysed videos across five platforms.

**Does this measure followers gained or views?**
Views, relative to the creator's normal video. A separate check on one account found that follow asks also raised follows per thousand views on TikTok and Instagram, which is why we trust the direction.

**Why measure against the creator's normal video instead of raw views?**
Raw views mostly measure account size. Dividing by the creator's own median isolates what the ask contributed.

**Is a comment ask bad?**
No. It is a normal video (1.03x). It just is not the lift most advice promises, and at the end of the video it lands on people who already left.
