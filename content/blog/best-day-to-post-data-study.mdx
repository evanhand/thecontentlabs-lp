Every "best time to post" chart on the internet is built from the same two or three recycled surveys. We built one from videos.

We pulled **4,959 short-form videos** from **76 creators** in The Content Labs dataset, took the exact posting timestamp of each one, and compared every video against that same creator's normal views. Measuring against the creator's own median is what makes this fair: a big account's Sunday does not outweigh a small account's Sunday, and a 1.0x always means "a normal video for this person." All times are US Eastern.

<Highlight title="Sunday beats Monday by a third">
Sunday posts ran at **1.25x** the creator's normal views. Monday posts ran at **0.92x**. That is the whole spread of the week, and it lines up with the time of day too: **morning** posts (5 to 11am) ran at **1.15x**, while posts after **9pm** ran at **0.98x** and doubled only 21.7% of the time, the lowest breakout rate of any slot.
</Highlight>

Three things stand out.

**The weekend is real.** Sunday (1.25x, 406 videos) and Saturday (1.18x, 534 videos) are the top two days, and they also have the top two breakout rates: 36.0% and 36.9% of weekend posts doubled the creator's normal, and 18.7% and 18.2% did five times it.

**Wednesday is the weekday to use.** It ran at 1.14x across 816 videos from 29 creators, the broadest sample of any day. Tuesday (1.08x) is a reasonable second. Thursday (0.97x) and Monday (0.92x) are below normal.

**Monday is a coin flip, not a dead day.** Its median is the lowest of the week, but 16.0% of Monday posts did 5x, better than Tuesday (13.5%), Wednesday (14.5%), Thursday (12.8%) or Friday (13.4%). Monday produces more ordinary videos and about the same number of big ones.

Morning wins on every measure. Posts between 5 and 11am ran at 1.15x across 1,437 videos, doubled 34.6% of the time, and did 5x 17.6% of the time. Midday (1.06x, 1,469 videos) and evening (1.06x, 896 videos) are fine. The afternoon dip to 0.98x is small.

The slot to avoid is after 9pm. Those 226 videos ran at 0.98x, doubled only 21.7% of the time, and did 5x only 10.2% of the time. A video posted at midnight is competing for a feed that will be rebuilt by morning.

## Why the weekend works

Part of it is competition. The creators in this dataset posted roughly half as often on weekends (406 Sunday posts versus 888 Thursday posts), so each weekend video shares the audience with fewer of its own siblings. Part of it is almost certainly watch time: an audience with a free morning finishes a two-minute video, and finished videos are the ones the platform keeps pushing. Our length study found videos over two minutes ran at 1.29x, and those are easier to finish on a Sunday than on a Monday commute.

<Callout title="The practical rule">
If you post once a day, post in the morning. If you post four times a week, make two of them Saturday and Sunday. If you have one video you believe in, hold it for Sunday morning rather than publishing it the moment it is done on Monday night.
</Callout>

## Caveats

These are one dataset's numbers, from creators who skew toward commentary, sports and news. A niche with a Monday-morning audience (business, productivity) may run the other way. The right use of this chart is as a default, and the right next step is to check your own week: if your Thursdays beat everyone's Sundays, trust your Thursdays.

## How we ran this

Every video was processed by The Content Labs analysis engine. We took the posting timestamp, converted it to US Eastern, and bucketed by weekday and by hour band. We then divided each video's views by that creator's median views over the period, so the numbers are relative to each creator's normal rather than raw counts. Buckets with fewer than 60 videos are not reported. The dataset is 9,289 analysed videos from 76 creators across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X; 4,959 had a posting time and a measured baseline.

## FAQ

**How big was the dataset?**
4,959 short-form videos with a posting time, from 76 creators on The Content Labs, drawn from 9,289 analysed videos across five platforms.

**What time zone are these in?**
US Eastern. Most creators in the dataset are US based. If your audience is elsewhere, shift the hour bands to their morning.

**Why measure against the creator's normal video instead of raw views?**
Raw views mostly measure account size. Dividing by the creator's own median isolates what the posting slot contributed.

**Does the day matter more than the video?**
No. The spread across days is 0.92x to 1.25x. The spread across content types in the same dataset is wider than that. Post the right video on the right day; the day alone will not save a weak one.
