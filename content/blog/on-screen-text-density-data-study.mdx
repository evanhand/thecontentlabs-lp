Short-form is watched on mute, in a feed, at speed. Everything you say has to also be seen. We measured how much that is worth.

We pulled **5,012 short-form videos** from **76 creators** in The Content Labs dataset, had the analysis engine rate the density of on-screen text in each one (none, light, heavy), and compared each video against that same creator's normal views. Measuring against the creator's own median keeps a big account from drowning out a small one; a 1.0x always means "a normal video for this person."

<Highlight title="The more the better">
Videos with **heavy** on-screen text ran at **1.22x** the creator's normal views across 857 videos from 29 creators, with **38.2%** doubling and **19.3%** doing five times it. **Light** text ran at **1.03x**. **No** text at all ran at **0.78x** across 238 videos, with only 23.5% doubling. The gap between heavy and none is 44 points of a normal video.
</Highlight>

Light text is the default (3,917 videos) and it buys you a normal video. Heavy text is rarer (857 videos) and it is where the breakouts live. No text is rare (238 videos) and it is the worst bucket in this study.

Heavy here means the text is doing real work: the key stat on screen when it is said, the name of the thing being discussed, the claim in a title card, the quote from the screenshot. It does not mean a wall of words over the creator's face.

The common thread is proof. A video that says "his numbers this season are insane" is a claim. A video that says it while the stat line sits on screen is evidence, and evidence gets screenshotted, shared and argued over.

## Why heavy text wins

Three reasons, all visible in the rest of the dataset.

**Sound off.** A feed viewer decides in the first second or two whether to turn the sound on. Text gives them a reason. The hook overlay number (1.07x versus 0.84x) is that decision in one line.

**Specificity.** On-screen text forces a specific number or name, and specific videos ran at 1.20x (grade 5) against 0.89x (grade 3) in the same dataset. Vague claims are hard to put on screen.

**Rewatches and shares.** A stat on screen is a screenshot waiting to happen. Screenshot-heavy videos had a 5x rate of 19.7%, among the highest of any bucket we measured.

<Callout title="The practical rule">
Every number you say, put on screen as you say it. Every name, every quote, every score. Put the hook on screen in the first frame. If the video has no number, name or quote worth showing, that is a note about the script, not the editing.
</Callout>

## Caveats

The creators here skew toward commentary, sports and news, where stats and screenshots are the native evidence. A vlog or a cooking video has fewer numbers to show, and "heavy" would look different there. The direction is very unlikely to flip; the size of the gap might shrink.

## How we ran this

Every video was processed by The Content Labs analysis engine, which watches the video and rates on-screen text density (none, light, heavy), whether the hook carries a text overlay, whether screenshots or clips appear, and the caption style, using one definition per label for every video. We then divided each video's views by that creator's median views over the period, so the numbers are relative to each creator's normal rather than raw counts. Buckets with fewer than 60 videos are not reported. The dataset is 9,289 analysed videos from 76 creators across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X; 5,012 had a text-density rating and a measured baseline.

## FAQ

**How big was the dataset?**
5,012 short-form videos with a text-density rating, from 76 creators on The Content Labs, drawn from 9,289 analysed videos across five platforms.

**What counts as heavy text?**
Text that carries the argument: stats, names, quotes and claims on screen as they are said. Light is a hook overlay and little else. None is a bare video.

**Why measure against the creator's normal video instead of raw views?**
Raw views mostly measure account size. Dividing by the creator's own median isolates what the editing contributed.

**Should I add text to every video?**
Add the text the video has a reason for. The data rewards evidence on screen, not decoration.
