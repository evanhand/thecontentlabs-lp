"Always put a specific number in your hook." You've heard it from every content guru. We ran it against **14,424 real TikTok and Instagram videos** to see what a number is actually worth.

The method, same as our other hook studies: every spoken hook in the dataset was checked for digits, and every video was measured against its own account's typical views so account size couldn't tilt the answer.

<Highlight title="The 5% hack">
Hooks with a number: **7.77x** the account's typical views. Hooks without: **7.42x**. The most repeated hook hack in short-form is worth about **5%**, within breathing distance of noise. For comparison, in the same dataset, [getting your hook to 9-12 words](/blog/hook-length-data-study) was worth 37% over the long-hook band, and [saying "you" instead of "I"](/blog/you-vs-i-hooks-data-study) was worth 56%.
</Highlight>

Hooks with dollar amounts or percentages averaged **6.83x**, below hooks with no number at all. "How I made $10K in 30 days" framing has been run so hard for so long that it now pattern-matches to an ad, and viewers treat it like one. Ordinary numbers doing honest work ("80% of all autoimmune diseases happen to women") still slightly outperform, because the number is the story rather than the bait.

## Why the myth survives

The number hack is easy to teach, easy to check, and it photographs well in a slide deck. Whether a hook opens a real gap is harder to teach. So the checkable thing became the advice, even though the data says it's a rounding error.

<Callout title="Where your effort actually pays">
In this same 14,424-video dataset: hook length done right was worth 37%, question phrasing 43%, viewer-first wording 56%. A number is worth 5%. Fix the sentence before you decorate it.
</Callout>

## How we ran this

Hooks are the spoken opening line from each video's transcript, extracted by The Content Labs analysis engine. A hook counted as "with number" if it contained any digit; the money group additionally contained a dollar sign, percent sign, or the words percent or dollars. Views were measured against each account's typical video, and accounts with fewer than 5 videos were excluded.

## FAQ

**Is a 5% difference literally nothing?**
It's real but tiny, and it's an average across 14,424 videos. It means the presence of a number, by itself, tells you almost nothing about whether a hook will work.

**Why would money numbers hurt?**
Audiences have seen a decade of income-claim content. Dollar-amount hooks now signal "you're being sold something", and the scroll reflex fires before the video can prove otherwise.

**What should I do with a hook I've already written?**
Check the things that moved the data: is it one complete, specific, unresolved idea of 9-16 words? Is it about the viewer? Would a question open the loop harder? The number can stay if it survives those questions.
