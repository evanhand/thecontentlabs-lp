Every video The Content Labs analyzes gets its hook graded, A+ through F, on effectiveness, creativity, and attention-grabbing potential. That gave us a question we could actually answer with data: **do hook grades match what the algorithm pays?**

We pulled every graded video with real view data: **2,485 videos across 24 accounts**, each measured against its own account's typical views so account size couldn't skew the comparison.

The rubric and the algorithm agree violently.

<Highlight title="The grade gap">
A-tier hooks (A+ and A) averaged **10.14x** the account's typical views. B-tier averaged **1.66x**. C-tier: **1.67x**. D and F hooks averaged **0.77x**, meaning a bad hook made the video perform **worse than that account's ordinary video**. And the A+ grade alone, the rubric's highest bar, averaged **42.7x** across 238 videos.
</Highlight>

Two things jump out of that chart.

**First, the cliff is between A and everything else.** B-tier and C-tier hooks performed nearly identically (1.66x vs 1.67x). The algorithm doesn't hand out partial credit for a decent hook. A hook either stops the scroll or it doesn't, and "pretty good" lands on the wrong side of that line. This matches what we found in our [hook length](/blog/hook-length-data-study) and [question hook](/blog/question-hooks-data-study) studies: the winning mechanics are specific, and near misses get paid like misses.

**Second, bad hooks are actively expensive.** D and F hooks didn't just underperform the A hooks. They underperformed the account's own normal output, flopping below half of typical views **43.5% of the time**. Whatever those videos cost to shoot and edit, the first sentence spent it before anyone watched.

## Consistency, not just ceiling

The averages could theoretically hide a lottery. They don't:

An A-grade hook was roughly **three times more likely to produce a breakout** than a B-grade hook. The grade isn't measuring polish. It's measuring the probability that the video gets a chance at all.

<Callout title="What this means for your next video">
Grade the hook before you shoot the video. If the opening line can't clear the A bar (one specific, unresolved idea, opened fast, aimed at the viewer), fixing it is worth more than anything you'll do in the edit. The data says the difference between a B and an A is the difference between 1.7x and 10x.
</Callout>

## How we ran this

Every video was analyzed by The Content Labs engine, which watches the video, reads the transcript, and grades the hook A+ through F on effectiveness, creativity, and attention-grabbing potential as part of a 30-dimension breakdown. We then compared each grade tier's views against the posting account's typical video. Accounts with fewer than 5 videos were excluded. Grades and views come from the same analysis pipeline, so treat this as the rubric agreeing with reality at scale rather than a blind forecasting test.
