Everyone says controversy gets views. Nobody says how much controversy, or how much it pays. We graded it.

We pulled **5,019 short-form videos** from **76 creators** in The Content Labs dataset. The analysis engine graded each video's central claim on a 1 to 5 controversy scale: 1 means nobody could object, 3 means some people will push back, 5 means half the audience will argue in the comments. We then compared each video against that same creator's normal views, so a big account's hot take and a small account's hot take are measured on the same scale.

<Highlight title="Flat, flat, flat, bend">
Level 1 videos ran at **0.93x** the creator's normal. Levels 2 and 3 ran at **1.03x** and **1.01x**, a normal video. Level 4 ran at **1.18x**. Level 5 ran at **1.63x**, with **43.4%** of them doubling the creator's normal and **22.8%** doing five times it. That is the largest gap between the top and bottom of any single feature we measured.
</Highlight>

The shape is the finding. A mild take does nothing: 819 level-2 videos and 1,233 level-3 videos both landed within three percent of a normal video. The lift starts at level 4 (1,205 videos, 34.4% doubling) and nearly triples at level 5. In plain terms, the audience does not reward "some people might disagree." It rewards "I know a lot of you are going to disagree, and here is why you are wrong."

The breakout rates make the same point. The share of videos that did 5x the creator's normal: 10.0% at level 1, 14.0% at level 2, 15.5% at level 3, 16.8% at level 4, 22.8% at level 5.

## Tone helps, but less

A controversial claim and an angry delivery are different things, and the data separates them.

Put together: the claim does more work than the voice. A level 5 claim delivered calmly still carries the argument. An outraged delivery of a level 2 claim is just loud.

## What a level 5 claim looks like

It is specific, it names a side, and it leaves no room to nod along. Our specificity grade confirms it: videos graded 5 on specificity ran at 1.20x and 4s at 1.15x, while 3s ran at 0.89x. A vague take ("the league is in a bad place") cannot be argued with. A specific one ("this player is the most overrated in the league and these three numbers prove it") can, and the comments do the distribution.

<Callout title="The practical rule">
Before you post a take, ask: could someone who loves this topic be angry at me for it? If the honest answer is no, you are at level 2 and the data says you are posting a normal video. Sharpen the claim until someone will argue, then back it with the number that makes the argument worth having.
</Callout>

## The cost

The curve has a downside the median hides. Level 5 is a wider distribution in both directions: more breakouts and more comments you will not enjoy. Level 1 videos almost never break out (10.0% at 5x) but they also do not start fights. For a creator whose business is a personal brand that needs to be liked, the gap between 0.93x and 1.63x may not be worth it. For a commentary account, it is the whole job.

The sample also skews toward commentary, sports and news, where arguing is the format. A level 5 claim in a cooking niche may land differently. The direction of the curve is almost certainly the same; the size of the bend may not be.

## How we ran this

Every video was processed by The Content Labs analysis engine, which watches the video, reads the transcript and grades the central claim on a 1 to 5 controversy scale using one fixed definition for every video. We then divided each video's views by that creator's median views over the period, so the numbers are relative to each creator's normal rather than raw counts. Buckets with fewer than 60 videos are not reported. The dataset is 9,289 analysed videos from 76 creators across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X; 5,019 had a controversy grade and a measured baseline.

## FAQ

**How big was the dataset?**
5,019 short-form videos with a controversy grade, from 76 creators on The Content Labs, drawn from 9,289 analysed videos across five platforms.

**What does level 5 mean exactly?**
A central claim that roughly half the audience for that topic would argue with. Level 1 is a claim nobody could object to. The same definition is applied to every video, which is what makes the levels comparable.

**Why measure against the creator's normal video instead of raw views?**
Raw views mostly measure account size. Dividing by the creator's own median isolates what the claim contributed.

**Is this just saying "be angry"?**
No. Outraged tone ran at 1.29x; level 5 controversy ran at 1.63x. The claim matters more than the delivery.
